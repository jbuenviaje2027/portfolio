Project Overview
A card-based conversation tool built through a human-centered design process, prototyped and tested with founders across the StartX accelerator community.
Built with Team Jello (a team of 3) as part of Design 121 at Stanford's d.school, in partnership with StartX. I led the project as Head User Researcher.
The Challenge
How might we use the human-centered design process to design an innovation hub for the next generation of startup founders at Stanford Research Park?

Creating the Cards

Our cards were heavily inspired by the game We're Not Really Strangers, ranging from light reflection (Seed level) to deeper conversation (Bloom level), plus questions written directly from the experiences of startup founders.

Cards were designed in Figma, printed on cardstock, corner-cut for a rounded feel, then sprayed with lacquer for a refined finish. We also built a custom box with a "How to Play" card.

One of the more surprising outcomes was how quickly the founders' guards came down. Most of them spend their days pitching, networking, and performing a version of themselves built for a room full of investors or competitors. But once there was a vessel built strictly for connection, with no ask, no agenda, and nothing to prove, that professional mask seemed to just fall away. Conversations that started as polite small talk kept sliding into something more honest: grief, doubt, the parts of running a company nobody really talks about. It made the case that founders don't necessarily need more networking spaces. They need spaces that are explicitly not that.

06Takeaways
Rooting My Work in User Needs
My group and I were unfamiliar with the experience of startup founders. This project stemmed directly from the conversations we had with them, and the pain points we noticed.
Designing for Emotional Impact
This project taught me that meaningful design goes beyond solving logistical problems. It can create space for vulnerability, connection, and authentic human interaction.
Conversation as a Designed Experience
This project reframed dialogue itself as something that can be intentionally crafted through prompts, pacing, environment, and material interaction.
